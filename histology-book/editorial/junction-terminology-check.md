## 1. Term 1 — Tight junction / Zonula occludens → **EVIDENCE PASSES** ✅

**Book form: `اتصال مضبوط` — retained, now backed by evidence.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Document** | wasiweb.com — «حجروي اتصال، د اتصال ډولونه» (Cellular junction, and the types of junction) |

entirely. Confidence is MEDIUM rather than HIGH only because the attestation is a single source.

**Supporting (independent) Afghan source:** TolAfghan, «هستولوژي Histology — دوهمه برخه»
(tolafghan.com/articles/19376) covers the junctional complex and names it
«2 **Tight Junction = Zonula Occludens**».

No change to the book text is required — the chapter already uses *Tight junction / Zonula occludens*
alongside the Dari term.

**No book-text change is required:** Chapter 4 already writes *Zonula adherens / Adherens junction /

**Decision owed by you:** adopt the international name as canonical (which is what the two Afghan

## 5. A correction to my own earlier reports — two overstatements

Reading the sources directly for this check turned up two statements in
`editorial/terminology-correction-report.md` and `final-terminology-evidence-gate.md` that are **too
strong**. I am reporting them rather than quietly leaving them, and I have **not** changed either term,
because you said not to make other changes.

**(a) "کاربوهایدریت appears in NO Afghan source" — that is WRONG.**
TolAfghan, «فزیولوژی حجره» (tolafghan.com/posts/30498, محمدشفیق محمدی) — the same Afghan source this
project already cites for مایتوکاندریا، انزایم، فسفوریلیشن، پروتئین — writes:

> «… و سایر لیپیدها ۴ فیصد و **کاربوهایدریت‌ها** ۳ فیصد.»

So the Afghan-pattern transliteration **کاربوهایدریت does appear in an Afghan source**. Your directive #3
said to keep `Carbohydrate` English unless *a reliable Afghan source establishes `کربوهیدرات`*. That
condition is still not met — no Afghan source uses `کربوهیدرات` — so **`Carbohydrate` stays
English-retained**, and I made no change. But the evidence picture is different from what my earlier
report claimed, and it is now yours to re-decide.

**(b) "اندامک was the Iranian form" — half right.**
The same TolAfghan page writes «به اجزای درون حجره **اندامک** گفته می‌شود». So `اندامک` is used by an
Afghan author too, alongside the official MoE Grade-7 textbook's «**ارگانل** (Organelle)». The
`ارگانل` decision still stands, on **institutional precedence** (an official Ministry of Education
curriculum outranks an individual author's article) — but not on the claim that `اندامک` is un-Afghan.
If you want `اندامک` registered as an accepted Afghan variant rather than forbidden, that is a decision
for you; I have left it forbidden.

This is also a caution about this project's footing generally: *TolAfghan is an Afghan source but its
register is mixed* — the same page contains `حجره` and `سلول`, `انزایم` and `انزیم`, `مایتوکاندریا`
and `مایتوکندریا`. It is good evidence of *what an Afghan author uses*, and weaker evidence of *what
Afghan institutions standardise on*. The institutional tier (MoPH, MoHE, MoE textbooks, medical
faculties) should keep outranking it.
